Great question. Based on my thorough review of RawDrive's gallery and knowing the competitive landscape (Pixieset, ShootProof, Pic-Time, Pass, Cloudspot, SmugMug, Sprout Studio), here's a detailed breakdown:

---

## 🔍 What RawDrive Has That's Already Strong

Before gaps, it's worth noting RawDrive already does several things competitors struggle with — **client-side encryption**, **tethered shooting built into the gallery**, **face search**, and **integrated CRM/delivery/sales in one workspace**. These are genuine differentiators.

---

## 🟡 Missing Features Compared to Competitors

### 📸 Client Experience (Guest/End User Side)

**Proofing / Selection workflow with feedback loop** — Pixieset and ShootProof allow clients to select favorites with a typed note per photo ("I love this one but can we crop it?"). RawDrive shows a selection limit number but there's no comment-per-selection or feedback-per-photo flow visible. Clients need a way to communicate *why* they picked or rejected something.

**Client download packages** — Competitors let photographers define download tiers: "full resolution", "web size", "print-ready with colour profile". RawDrive currently appears to offer a single download toggle (on/off). Tiered download packages are table-stakes for pros.

**Print store / product ordering** — Pic-Time and SmugMug have deeply integrated print fulfilment where clients can order prints, canvases, albums directly from the gallery. RawDrive has a "Sales" tab but it only shows linked records — there's no visible storefront, product catalogue, or fulfilment pipeline.

**Slideshow / autoplay mode** — Most competitors offer a full-screen slideshow clients can play with music. This is a huge emotional touchpoint for wedding and portrait photographers. Vimeo/YouTube embed is a partial workaround, but a native photo slideshow with background music upload would be far stronger.

**Gallery expiry / access window** — ShootProof and Pixieset let photographers set "this gallery expires in 30 days". No expiry setting was visible in Settings. This creates urgency for clients to download and protects the photographer's storage.

**Mobile app for clients** — Competitors like Pic-Time have dedicated iOS/Android apps where clients get push notifications when galleries are ready, can download directly, and browse offline. RawDrive has a "Install App" (PWA) for the studio owner, but no client-facing mobile app story.

---

### 💬 Communication & Notifications

**Automated email delivery to client** — Pixieset and ShootProof have built-in email templates: "Your gallery is ready!", reminder emails, and expiry warnings, all sent automatically from inside the platform. RawDrive has an "Email share link" button which appears to compose a manual email. A branded, automated email drip (gallery live → reminder on day 7 → "last chance" on day 25) would save photographers enormous admin time.

**Client activity notifications for photographer** — "Your client Germany viewed the gallery for the first time", "3 photos were added to favorites", "client downloaded 47 photos" — these real-time owner notifications aren't visible in the Delivery dashboard (it only shows aggregate counts for the last 30 days, not per-event alerts).

**Gallery comments visible to both sides** — The comment feature exists in the lightbox but appears owner-only. Competitors like Pass and Pic-Time have a threaded client-photographer conversation per photo, which is essential for revision rounds.

---

### 🎨 Design & Branding

**Custom domain for client galleries** — `yourstudio.rawdrive.in/gallery/client-name` vs the current `rawdrive.in/g/test-xxxx`. Pixieset, Pic-Time, and ShootProof all allow CNAME mapping so clients see the photographer's own brand in the URL. This is a top request from professional photographers.

**Logo & brand customisation on the gallery** — No visible option to add the photographer's logo, tagline, or brand colours to the public gallery header. Competitors allow full white-labelling. The gallery currently shows "UAT Test Gallery" with no photographer identity visible.

**Custom accent colour / theme** — Only dark/light toggle visible. Competitors let you set a hex colour for buttons, links, and highlights to match the photographer's brand identity.

**Gallery intro / cover video** — Pixieset Pro lets photographers put a short looping video (reel) as the gallery hero instead of a still image. For wedding and commercial photographers this creates a far more premium first impression.

**"Thank you" page after download** — A branded page that appears after the client downloads ("Thank you! Please leave us a Google review" with a link). Highly effective for referrals and completely absent from most tools, but Pic-Time has it.

---

### 📁 File & Asset Management

**RAW file delivery** — RawDrive handles encrypted upload via the desktop app, but the client-side download of RAW files isn't explicitly supported or surfaced. Some photographers deliver RAW + JPEG pairs; ShootProof handles this cleanly.

**Version history / re-upload** — If a photographer edits a photo and wants to replace it (e.g. after client feedback), there's no visible "replace photo" option per image. Competitors support version swap in place, keeping the same URL.

**Smart album / auto-grouping** — Competitors like Pic-Time use AI to auto-group photos by scene (ceremony, reception, details, portraits). With RawDrive already having face detection, this would be a natural extension — auto-create sub-galleries by detected faces or EXIF timestamp clusters.

**Bulk metadata editing** — Adding keywords, copyright info, or captions to multiple photos at once. No visible batch metadata editor.

---

### 🛒 Business & Monetisation

**Package / pricing tiers per gallery** — Sprout Studio lets you define what's included in each package (e.g. "50 digital downloads, 1 canvas print, gallery access for 90 days") and enforce it at the gallery level. RawDrive's "Sales" tab links to external records but doesn't enforce delivery scope.

**Upsell prompts inside the gallery** — When a client tries to download more photos than their package allows, competitors show a tasteful upsell: "You've used 10 of 10 downloads — upgrade to get all 94 photos for ₹2,999". This turns the gallery itself into a sales channel.

**Referral / affiliate tracking** — A unique link that tracks which clients came from referrals. Pic-Time has this; it rewards photographers who bring new users and clients who refer friends.

---

### 🔒 Security & Access Control

**Per-email access control** — Rather than a single gallery password, some photographers want to invite specific email addresses (like a bride and her mother separately). ShootProof supports individual guest access links with separate tracking per recipient.

**Download watermark (visual)** — The watermark toggle exists in Settings but it appears to only apply to gallery display images, not to the downloadable files. Competitors can optionally bake a subtle watermark into the actual downloaded JPEG for proofing rounds.

**GDPR / data deletion request workflow** — No visible client data deletion button. As RawDrive targets Indian photographers but serves an international market, a "request data deletion" flow for clients would be important for compliance.

---

## 🏆 Features That Would Keep RawDrive at #1

These are things that, if built well, no competitor currently does cleanly:

**1. AI-powered culling suggestions** — Given face detection is already running on upload, RawDrive could surface "we detected 14 near-duplicate frames of this moment — here are our top 3" to help photographers cull faster before gallery delivery. No competitor does AI-culling inside the delivery platform.

**2. Real-time collaborative proofing (like Figma for photos)** — Photographer and client in the same gallery view simultaneously, with a cursor-like presence indicator. Photographer can annotate ("I recommend this one") and client responds in real time. A complete category differentiator.

**3. Encrypted RAW delivery** — Since RawDrive already does client-side encryption (a unique moat), extending that to RAW file delivery to the client would be industry-first. No other photo delivery platform encrypts in transit at the source — RawDrive could market this hard to commercial and legal/forensic photographers.

**4. One-tap WhatsApp delivery** — In India specifically, clients expect gallery links over WhatsApp, not email. A "Send on WhatsApp" button with a pre-composed message template would be a massive differentiator for the Indian photography market. None of the Western competitors (Pixieset, ShootProof) have this.

**5. Offline gallery (downloadable PWA)** — Allow clients to download the entire gallery as a local PWA/app that works without internet. Incredibly useful in India's variable connectivity conditions and completely unique to the market.

**6. AI caption generation** — Auto-generate social-media-ready captions for each photo using the image content. Client can one-tap copy the caption and post directly. Free social media value for the client = higher perceived value of the gallery.

**7. Studio analytics dashboard** — A cross-gallery analytics view: which galleries get the most views, which photos get the most downloads, which clients engage the least (at risk of churn), average time to proofing completion. None of the competitors surface this intelligently.

**8. Testimonial / review collection built into the gallery** — After a client downloads their photos, a subtle "Love your photos? Leave us a review" prompt with one-click links to Google and JustDial. Photographers in India live and die by referrals, and automating this touchpoint inside the gallery workflow is a significant business-value add.
